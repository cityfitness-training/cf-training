01 What Is B+F Logic — And Why It Works Foundation ▼

B+F stands for Benefit plus Frequency. It is a structured approach to connecting the amenities a guest is excited about with how often they will realistically use them — then using those numbers to justify the membership tier that covers everything they want.

Here is why it works so powerfully: most price objections happen because the guest is comparing the cost of the membership to an abstract number in their head. B+F replaces that abstract number with a concrete, personalized value equation built entirely from their own words.

The Shift B+F Creates
Without B+F: "Is $79.50 bi-weekly worth it?" — abstract, easy to second-guess.

With B+F: "Based on using the pool 4x a month, Revive after every workout, and 3 studio classes a week — does Performance+ make sense?" — concrete, personal, and built from their commitments.

The guest cannot argue with the second version without contradicting themselves. That is the power of B+F. You are not selling a price. You are confirming a solution they already described.

The Three Goals of B+F
1. Increase the percentage of guests who choose Performance+ or Studio+.   2. Give you a data-backed justification for the tier you recommend.   3. Create the most powerful objection-handling tool you have — their own stated frequency — before the objection ever arrives.
02 Plant Premium Seeds Early — During the Tour Timing ▼

B+F Logic does not start at the desk. It starts the moment you begin the tour. Every time you show a premium amenity — Revive, the Pool Club, Studio classes, multi-club access — and you gauge their interest, you are laying the groundwork for the frequency conversation that follows.

The tour is where you plant the seed. The desk review is where you harvest it.

How to Plant the Seed on the Tour
At each premium amenity, pause and ask a light frequency question. Not the full B+F review yet — just a casual gauge of interest: "How often do you see yourself taking advantage of something like this?" Their answer is a data point you hold onto. Write it down.

Consultant"If you had access to this every single day, how often do you think you'd take advantage of it?"
Guest"Honestly, probably after every workout. Maybe 4 or 5 times a week."

AT STUDIOGuest comments on the class schedule board.

Guest"Two or three probably."

By the time you reach the desk, you have three data points — all from the guest. You have not mentioned price once. You have not pushed Performance+. You have simply listened and noted. The review almost writes itself.

03 The Tour Review — Setting Up the B+F Recap at the Desk The Transition ▼

As you transition from the floor back to the desk, you set up the B+F recap with a simple, conversational bridge. This is not a formal presentation moment — it is a natural continuation of the tour conversation.

Tour Review Bridge Script
"Hey [Name], before we look at memberships — let's do a quick recap of what we saw and make sure I'm recommending the right fit for you. Sound good?"

This framing does three things:

It positions you as a consultant, not a closer. "Make sure I'm recommending the right fit" sounds like genuine service — because it is. You are not presenting options and waiting for them to pick. You are prescribing a specific solution based on what they told you.

It signals that a recommendation is coming. The guest knows they are about to be told which membership fits them — not handed a menu. This reduces decision fatigue and positions you as the expert.

It creates a natural yes. "Sound good?" is a micro tie-down that gets their implicit agreement to proceed. They have consented to hear the recap — which means they have consented to hearing the recommendation that follows.

Do Not Skip the Recap
Going straight from tour to price card without the B+F review is one of the most common mistakes in the close sequence. The recap is what converts tour excitement into membership justification. Skip it and you are presenting a price without a foundation.
04 The Frequency Question — Getting Them to Commit The Core Skill ▼

The frequency question is the engine of B+F Logic. It is a simple, direct ask for each premium amenity: how often would you use this? The answer becomes the justification for the tier you recommend.

Here is the cadence for each amenity during the desk review:

Pool Club
"You mentioned the pool was something you could really benefit from. How often would you realistically use it per month?"
Even 4x per month at a local pool drop-in rate (often $10–$15/visit) is $40–$60 in value from this amenity alone.
Revive Suite
"You seemed really excited about Revive. If you had access every day, how many times a week do you think you'd actually use it?"
A single infrared sauna session at a standalone spa runs $30–$60. Four uses a month = $120–$240 in standalone value.
Studio Classes
"How many classes per week do you realistically see yourself taking? Even a conservative estimate."
Boutique studio classes average $25–$35 each. Two per week = $200–$280/month in standalone cost.
Multi-Club Access
"You mentioned [their location/commute]. How often do you think you'd take advantage of visiting a different location?"
Even occasional multi-club use adds massive schedule flexibility — especially for members who travel within the city or work from different offices.
Listen and Write
When they answer, write it down in front of them. This communicates that their answer matters and has just become the basis for your recommendation. It also prevents any "I didn't say that" moments later — because they watched you record it.
05 The Recap and Recommendation — Closing the Loop The Prescription ▼

Once you have gathered their frequency commitments for each amenity, you summarize their numbers back to them and deliver your recommendation as a logical conclusion — not a sales pitch. This is the moment B+F pays off.

The B+F Recap and Recommendation Script
"Okay — so based on everything we talked about: you said you'd use the pool about 4–6 times a month, Revive after most of your workouts which puts you at probably 3–4 times a week, and you'd realistically take 2–3 studio classes a week. That's a lot of access — and it all points to one membership: Performance+. This is the only tier that covers all of it without you having to think twice about what's included. Does that make sense based on what you told me?"

Notice the structure: their numbers → your conclusion → confirmation question. You are not asking if they want Performance+. You are confirming that it logically follows from their own words. The closing question is not "what do you think?" — it is "does that make sense?" The distinction is everything.

One Option First
Always recommend one membership — the right one — based on their frequency data. Do not present all three and let them choose. You are the expert. You are prescribing the solution. Presenting options before you have been unable to close on Performance+ tells the guest there is a cheaper answer available and invites them to find it.

The Neighborhood Rule
Present Neighborhood only if the guest has explicitly and repeatedly made clear they want basic access only and have no interest in premium benefits — even after you have presented the value. Leading with Neighborhood or offering it proactively leaves significant value and revenue on the table. It is a last resort, not a starting point.
Studio+ Key Fact

When Performance+ is genuinely not the fit, Studio+ is your strong alternative — not Neighborhood. Lead with the value of multi-club access and class inclusion before ever mentioning Neighborhood.
07 B+F as Your Best Objection Weapon The Payoff ▼

Here is where B+F Logic pays its biggest dividend. When a price objection arrives — "Performance+ is a bit more than I was thinking" — you do not need to defend the price. You simply return to their numbers.

The objection is about price. Your response is about value — specifically, the value they calculated themselves during the tour review. You are not arguing. You are reminding.

B+F in Objection Handling — Full Example
Guest"I think Performance+ might be a little more than I was planning to spend."
Consultant"I completely hear that — and I want to make sure we're making the right decision for you. Can I just walk you back through the numbers for a second?"
Guest"Sure."
Consultant"You told me you'd use Revive probably 4–5 times a week — that's a recovery spa session most people pay $30–$60 for individually. You said the pool was something you'd use 4–6 times a month. And 2–3 studio classes a week at a boutique studio runs $25–$35 each. When you add those up outside of a membership, you are easily looking at $300–$400+ a month in standalone costs. Performance+ covers all of that at $79.50 bi-weekly — which is roughly $40 a week. Does it still feel like too much when you look at it that way?"
Guest"When you put it like that... no, not really."
The Golden Rule of B+F Objection Handling
You are never arguing about price. You are reminding them of what they said — and showing them what their own usage is actually worth. That is a completely different conversation. One you win because the data came from them.

Common Mistakes
What Breaks the Logic

6 B+F Mistakes
& How to Fix Them

✗ The Mistake
Skipping the tour review and going straight from the tour to the price card. You have no foundation. You are presenting a number with no context.
✓ The Fix
Always do the B+F recap at the desk before touching the price sheet. Even a 2-minute review changes the entire dynamic of how the price lands.
✗ The Mistake
Forgetting to plant frequency seeds during the tour. You get to the desk with no data — and have to ask cold frequency questions that feel like an interrogation.
✓ The Fix
Ask one light frequency question at every premium amenity stop. By the time you hit the desk, you have the data. The recap is a summary, not a new conversation.
✗ The Mistake
Presenting all three membership tiers at once: "We have Performance+, Studio+, and Neighborhood — which do you want?" You just handed them a reason to choose the cheapest one.
✓ The Fix
Recommend one tier — the right one based on their frequency data. Present it as the logical conclusion. Only introduce alternatives if they push back after LIGO.
✗ The Mistake
Not writing down their frequency answers. When you quote their numbers back during objection handling, you have no record — and they may dispute or downgrade what they said.
✓ The Fix
Write every frequency answer down in front of them the moment they say it. That visual commitment makes the number real — and makes it much harder to walk back later.
✗ The Mistake
Leading with Neighborhood to avoid price friction. You have assumed they cannot afford more — and denied yourself the chance to show them the value that would justify it.
✓ The Fix
Always lead with Performance+ if their frequency data supports it. Let the B+F math make the case. The guest will tell you if it genuinely does not work — and then you pivot with LIGO.
✗ The Mistake
During objection handling, arguing about the price instead of returning to their frequency commitments. You are on defense. They are on offense. You will lose.
✓ The Fix
Never argue price. Return to their numbers. "Let me walk you back through what you told me." Their own data is the only objection-handler that cannot be argued with.

Scenario 01 — High-Usage Guest, Clear Performance+ Straightforward
During the tour, the guest was visibly excited about Revive, asked multiple questions about the pool, and said they would love to try WE/FIT classes. Their frequency answers: Revive 4–5x/week, pool 6x/month, classes 2–3x/week.
Your objective: Execute the full B+F recap at the desk — summarize their numbers back to them, calculate the standalone value of their stated usage, and recommend Performance+ as the logical conclusion with confidence. Close with: "Based on what you told me, this is the one." Then flip to the price sheet.
This is the scenario B+F was built for. The data is clean, the recommendation is obvious, and your job is to present it with conviction. The most common mistake here is underselling the recommendation — being tentative when you should be certain. Their numbers justify it. Be the expert.
Scenario 02 — No Pool Interest, Moderate Class Usage Studio+ Decision
Guest has zero interest in the pool and does not swim. They loved the studio classes and showed interest in Revive — specifically the cryo chamber. Their frequency answers: classes 2x/week, cryo 2–3x/week, not interested in multi-club.
Your objective: B+F the Studio+ recommendation. Lead with the class value (boutique classes at $25–$35 each = $200–$280/month for 2 per week). Layer in cryo access. Emphasize the multi-club benefit even if they said they would not use it — frame it as the flexibility they did not know they wanted. Present Studio+ as the clear fit — not as a consolation prize.
Studio+ should be presented with the same conviction as Performance+. It is not the "cheaper version." It is the right version for a guest whose usage pattern does not include the pool. Members who join on Studio+ stay longer and report higher satisfaction — lead with that confidence.
Scenario 03 — Low Frequency Answers Across the Board Low-Commitment Guest
When asked frequency questions, the guest consistently hedges: "Maybe once a month for the sauna," "I probably wouldn't do classes," "I'd mostly just use the weights." Their answers point toward basic access only.
Your objective: Do not immediately drop to Neighborhood. First, probe gently: "Even conservatively — if you came 3 times a week and had recovery available after each workout, do you think you'd take advantage?" Try to elevate one amenity. If they genuinely do not engage with any premium feature after probing, you can present Neighborhood — but frame it as the starting point with the ability to upgrade, not the final destination.
Low frequency answers often reflect a confidence issue, not a genuine disinterest. The guest may not believe they will actually use the premium features because they have started and stopped before. Probe the Start & Stopper pattern: "What would make you feel like you'd actually show up consistently this time?" That answer may unlock a different frequency conversation entirely.
Scenario 04 — Price Objection After B+F Recap Objection Handling
You have executed a clean B+F recap, recommended Performance+, presented the price — and the guest says: "That's honestly a bit more than I budgeted for. I was thinking more like $50 bi-weekly."
Your objective: Do not flinch and immediately offer Studio+. First, return to their frequency data: "I hear you — can I walk you back through the numbers one more time?" Rebuild the standalone value equation using their usage commitments. Make the math visible. If after LIGO they are still firm, then — and only then — introduce Studio+ as a genuine alternative that still covers their core interests. Never open with the downgrade.
The budget objection after a clean B+F recap is usually not a real budget limit — it is a comfort issue. They are not sure the membership will stick, so the price feels risky. Returning to their frequency numbers reframes the question: this is not "is $79.50 a lot?" It is "is the value of everything you said you'd use worth $79.50?" That is a question they have already answered.
