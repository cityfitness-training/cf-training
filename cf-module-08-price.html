Price
Presentation.

This is the moment everything you have built culminates. The greeting, the discovery, the tour, B+F Logic, HACCL — all of it has been preparing for this. When you get here, you are not presenting a price. You are confirming a solution the guest has already described. This module teaches you to do it with precision, confidence, and — most importantly — silence.

The Objective

Ask. Present. Close.
Then Be Silent.

The price presentation is not a negotiation. It is not a guess. It is the logical conclusion of everything that came before it — delivered with confidence, structured with precision, and closed with one question followed by absolute silence. The consultant who masters that silence masters the close.

The Core Truth
Selling is a transfer of emotion. If you are confident, they become confident. If you fumble numbers, apologize for the price, or fill the silence after the close question — you transfer doubt. Confidence in the price presentation is not optional. It is the close itself.

01 The Pre-Presentation Checklist — Are You Ready? Before You Begin ▼

Before you reach for the laminated sheet, you must be able to answer yes to four questions. If any of these is a no, you are not ready — and presenting price before you are ready is one of the most expensive mistakes in the sales process.

✓
Does this guest have reason to like and trust me? — The relationship is the foundation. If you are not sure they trust you, they will not buy from you. A simple test: have you learned enough about them to genuinely like them? If yes, the trust is likely mutual. If not, the close is premature.
✓
Did we get yes answers to all five HACCL questions? — HACCL is your pre-close. If any letter produced hesitation that was not fully resolved, go back and address it before presenting price. An unresolved HACCL item will surface as an objection post-price and is twice as hard to handle there.
✓
Are there any outstanding objections that were mentioned earlier and not addressed? — Did they mention a partner needs to be consulted? A competing gym they are also considering? A health concern? These do not go away — they resurface at the worst possible moment. Address them before the price card.
✓
Do I have the prorated amounts calculated and ready? — Fumbling with a calculator after asking for the sale destroys confidence instantly. Know your numbers before you sit down. Have the prorated dues, the due-today total, the next billing date, and the annual facility fee date already calculated. Write them cleanly. Selling is a transfer of emotion — and that emotion is confidence.
If Any Answer Is No
Do not proceed to price. Identify which checklist item is incomplete and address it first. Presenting price before the foundation is solid does not save time — it creates objections you could have prevented.
02 One Option First — You Are the Expert The Recommendation ▼

You are not a menu. You are a prescription. Based on everything you learned in discovery and confirmed with B+F Logic, you know which membership is the right fit. Recommend that one — and only that one — first. Do not present all three tiers and let the guest choose.

When a doctor prescribes medication, they do not say "here are three options, pick the one you like best." They say "based on your symptoms and history, this is what I recommend." You are the fitness professional. The membership recommendation follows the same logic.

The Default Recommendation
Always lead with Performance+ if the guest's frequency data supports it — pool, Revive, studio classes, multi-club. It is the most comprehensive, highest-value membership and delivers the best member experience. When it is the right fit, present it as the only fit. Introduce alternatives only if Performance+ is genuinely not appropriate after LIGO.
The Recommendation Script
"Based on everything you shared with me today — and specifically based on how you said you'd use the pool, Revive, and the studio — there's really one membership that covers everything you described. It's called Performance+, and I want to walk you through exactly what's included."

Notice the structure: reference their specific usage → state the conclusion → transition to the features side. You are not pitching. You are confirming. The language "based on how you said you'd use it" is the key — it roots the recommendation in their words, not your preference.

03 Features Side First — ALWAYS. Never Start With Price. The Sheet ▼

The laminated price sheet has two sides: the Membership Features side and the Price side. This is not a coincidence — it is a sequence. You always begin on the Features side. Always. Without exception. Starting on the Price side is one of the most damaging mistakes in the presentation.

Here is why the order matters: when a guest sees a number before they fully understand what it covers, their brain anchors to the cost and evaluates everything through that lens. When they understand the full value of what they are getting first, the price lands in the context of everything it includes — which is where it belongs.

The Psychology of Features First
Value is built incrementally. Every feature you describe adds to the perceived worth of the membership. By the time you flip to the price side, the guest has mentally calculated what everything is worth — and the price you present is almost always less than what they expected. That gap between expectation and reality is where the close lives.
Line-by-Line With B+F Integration
As you walk through each feature on the features side, integrate the frequency questions from B+F Logic: "So if you woke up tomorrow with unlimited access to the recovery suite — how often would you use it per month?" Their answers reinforce the value in real time. By the end of the features side, they have re-stated their usage commitments and the tier is fully justified before the flip.
The Most Common Sheet Mistake
Flipping directly to the price side because "they seem ready" or "they already know what they want." It does not matter how engaged they are — always go through the features side first. You are not saving time by skipping it. You are removing the value foundation that makes the price defensible.
04 The Price Side — Walking Through Every Line The Numbers ▼

After the features side is complete, you flip to the price side. This is where precision matters. Every line item should be explained clearly and confidently — no hesitation, no apologizing for fees, no mumbling through numbers. Know them cold before you sit down.

1
Agreement Type
State the agreement length first. Performance+ is a 12-month agreement.
"The Performance+ is a 12-month agreement."
2
Bi-Weekly Dues
State the bi-weekly dues and point to the box. $79.50 bi-weekly for Performance+.
"Your bi-weekly dues are $79.50." [Point to the bi-weekly dues box.]
3
Next Billing Date
Write the next billing date in the box. This removes the anxiety of "when does my first payment come out?"
"Your next payment isn't due until [date]." [Write the date clearly.]
4
Annual Facility Fee
State the annual fee and the date it is due — 90 days from enrollment. Never bury this. Transparency here prevents a future cancellation triggered by a surprise charge.
"There's a standard Annual Facility Fee of $[X], which helps us maintain our clubs. That's due on [date — 90 days from today]." [Write the date.]
5
Enrollment Fee
State the enrollment fee clearly. For Performance+, enrollment is $0 — which is itself a strong selling point worth calling out.
"This membership has a $0 enrollment fee — all members pay it, and Performance+ waives it entirely." [Write $0.]
6
Prorated Dues
Explain prorates before writing the amount. A prorated charge that appears without explanation triggers confusion and resistance.
"Because your next full billing date isn't until [date], there's a small prorated amount due today — this just aligns your membership with our billing cycle. That comes to [prorated amount]." [Write it clearly.]
7
Due Today Total
Add enrollment fee plus prorated dues. Write the total in the Due Today box. This is the number the guest will see and focus on.
"So the enrollment fee of $[X] plus the prorated dues of $[X] equals your total due today: $[TOTAL]." [Write it clearly and confidently.]
05 The Close Question — One Line. Then Silence. The Most Important Moment ▼

You have walked through every feature. You have explained every line item on the price side. The due today total is written clearly. Now you ask one question — and you do not say another word until the guest speaks.

The Close Question — Exact Wording
"So, [Guest's Name], the total today is $[AMOUNT]. Did you want to use debit or credit?"

Then stop. Completely. Do not add anything. Do not explain. Do not soften. Do not fill the silence. The question has been asked. The next person who speaks loses.

The Silence.
After you ask the close question, you do not speak again until the guest does. Not to fill the silence. Not to help them think. Not to add one more benefit. The moment you speak, you have taken the pressure off — and invited doubt back into the room.
The next person who speaks loses.

Why the silence works: the close question creates a moment of productive tension. The guest is processing. Their silence is not a no — it is thinking. Every second you let that tension exist is a second closer to a yes. The moment you break the silence to help them, you have done their job for them — and the job they were about to do was say yes.

Why "Debit or Credit?" Instead of "Would You Like to Join?"
"Would you like to join?" is a yes/no question. It explicitly invites a no. "Did you want to use debit or credit?" is an assumptive close — it assumes membership and only asks about the payment method. Both answers (debit or credit) result in a membership. The question is not if — it is how.
Hold for at Least 30 Seconds
30 seconds of silence feels like an eternity. It is not. Practice holding silence in role-play until it feels comfortable. The consultant who can hold silence comfortably for 30+ seconds closes at a dramatically higher rate than one who fills it at 5 seconds.
06 Studio+ and Neighborhood — When and How to Present The Alternatives ▼

Performance+ is the default recommendation. But there are situations where Studio+ or Neighborhood is the genuinely appropriate fit — and when those situations arise, you need to know how to present each one with the same conviction and structure you would give Performance+.

Studio+ — When to Present It
Present Studio+ only when you are certain the guest has no interest in the Pool Club or full Revive suite — even after presenting the value of both. It should never feel like a consolation prize. Lead with what it includes: all studio classes, multi-club access across all 8 locations, Cryo and Hydromassage. Studio+ members report higher satisfaction and longer tenure than Neighborhood members — present it as a strong, comprehensive choice.
Studio+ Presentation Bridge
"Since you mentioned the pool and Revive aren't things you'd use regularly, I want to show you the membership that's actually the best fit for what you described. It's called Studio+ — and it gives you everything you told me you care about: all eight locations, every studio class, and recovery access through cryo and Hydromassage."
Neighborhood — Last Resort Only
Present Neighborhood only when a guest has explicitly and repeatedly refused all premium benefits after a full B+F review and LIGO application. It is basic facility access at their home club only — no classes, no multi-club, limited recovery. Never offer it proactively. It is an option of last resort, not a starting point.

Regardless of which tier you present, the structure is identical: features side first, line-by-line with B+F frequency questions, price side with every field explained, close question, silence. The framework does not change — only the membership details do.

07 Confidence Is the Product — Numbers Without Apology Delivery ▼

The way you present the price is as important as the price itself. A consultant who writes the due-today total slowly, pauses awkwardly, and then says "so... it's $XXX... but that includes everything..." has just communicated doubt. A consultant who writes it cleanly, looks up, and says "the total today is $XXX — debit or credit?" has communicated certainty.

The guest is not just evaluating the number. They are evaluating whether you believe the number is fair. Your delivery is the answer to that question before they even process the amount.

The Confidence Killers — What Never to Do
Never apologize for the price: "I know it's a little higher than some places, but..." — Never preemptively defend it: "It might seem like a lot, but when you consider everything..." — Never trail off after writing the total: the silence after writing should be your confident look-up, not an awkward pause. Never reach for the calculator visibly unless absolutely necessary — know your numbers cold.
The Look-Up Technique
After writing the due-today total, put the pen down. Look up from the sheet. Make direct eye contact. Then ask the close question. This sequence — write, pen down, look up, question — communicates complete confidence and readiness. Practice it until it feels completely natural in role-play.

Remember: a guest who asks "why is it so expensive?" after a confident presentation is giving you an opportunity. A guest who asks the same question after an apologetic one is expressing genuine doubt you planted yourself. Confidence is not arrogance. It is belief in what you are selling.

Role-Play Scenarios
Practice

4 Price Presentation
Scenarios to Train On

Scenario 01 — Clean Performance+ Close Standard
Guest completed HACCL with five clean yes answers. B+F review confirmed strong usage across pool, Revive, and studio. Pre-presentation checklist is all green. Prorates are calculated and ready.
Your objective: Execute the complete presentation — features side line-by-line with B+F frequency callbacks, smooth flip to the price side, every field explained and written clearly, close question, and hold the silence for a full 30 seconds without speaking. Focus specifically on the pen-down, look-up, eye contact sequence before the close question. The close question and the silence are the two things to drill hardest here.
Time yourself. The full features-side walkthrough plus price-side explanation should take 5–7 minutes. The close question should take 5 seconds. The silence after it should feel like 60 seconds — it will probably only be 15–20. That's fine. Hold it. The guest will speak first.
Scenario 02 — Guest Asks About Price Mid-Features Side Interruption
You are walking through the features side and the guest interrupts: "Before we go further — can you just tell me how much it is?"
Your objective: Do not flip to the price side. Acknowledge the question warmly and redirect: "I promise we're going to get there in about 60 seconds — I just want to make sure you see everything that's included first so the number makes complete sense in context. Bear with me for one more minute." Continue on the features side. Flip when you are ready — not when they push. Rewarding impatience with the price before value is built resets everything you just built.
This happens more often than you might expect. The guest is not being difficult — they are genuinely curious and slightly impatient. Your job is to buy 60 more seconds without making them feel dismissed. Warm acknowledgment plus a confident redirect works every time. Never say "I'll get to that" flatly — make it sound like you are doing them a favor by showing them the features first.
Scenario 03 — Silence Breaks — Guest Starts to Waffle Post-Close Pressure
You asked the close question and held the silence. After 20 seconds, the guest says: "I don't know... I think I need to think about it a little more."
Your objective: This is your transition into LIGO — do not react with disappointment or pushback. Listen fully. Identify: "It sounds like you want to make sure this is the right decision — I completely understand that." Give permission: "That's actually a really healthy instinct." Offer alternative: return to their X-Factor and the frequency commitments they made. "I just want to make sure — when you think about what you said earlier about wanting to feel like yourself again and finally having something that covers everything... what part of this feels unclear?" Draw out the real objection and handle it specifically.
The "I need to think about it" response after the close is one of the most common in the business. It almost never means what it says. It usually means: "I'm not 100% sure about something and I don't know exactly what it is." Your job through LIGO is to help them identify that something — and resolve it. The close question is not one and done. It can be asked again after LIGO resolution.
Scenario 04 — Numbers Fumble Recovery Confidence Under Pressure
You are mid-price-side and realize you have the prorated amount wrong. You need to recalculate in front of the guest. You can feel the energy in the room shift.
Your objective: Do not panic, over-apologize, or let the energy drop. Own it quickly and calmly: "Give me one second — I want to make sure I have your exact prorated amount right." Recalculate clearly. Write the corrected number. Continue without making the moment larger than it is. A quick, matter-of-fact correction from a confident consultant loses almost no ground. An apologetic, flustered correction loses everything. The recovery is the skill — practice it so it feels instinctive.
Number fumbles happen to everyone — even experienced consultants. What separates pros from beginners is recovery speed. One breath, a calm acknowledgment, a clean correction, and you are right back in flow. The guest takes their cues from you. If you treat it as a minor interruption, they will too. If you treat it as a crisis, they will as well.
