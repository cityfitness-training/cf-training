Pre-Tour
Discovery.

The tour is only as good as the discovery that comes before it. This is where you stop talking and start listening — uncovering the real reasons someone walked through the door so you can build everything that follows around what actually matters to them.

The Objective

Listen More Than You Talk.

The pre-tour is not a questionnaire. It is a conversation. Your job is to gather enough information about this person — their history, their goals, their emotional driver — to make every step of the tour feel like it was built specifically for them. Because it was.

The Core Standard
In a great pre-tour discovery, the guest does 70% of the talking. You do 30% — and most of that 30% is questions, not answers. The consultant who talks the most during discovery learns the least.

01 "Where Are You on Your Fitness Journey?" — The Perfect Opener The Starting Point ▼

The pre-tour starts with one question. Not a form. Not a checklist. One open, non-intimidating question that invites the guest to tell their own story in whatever way feels natural to them.

The Opening Question
"So tell me — where are you on your fitness journey right now?"

Why this question works so well:

It is non-judgmental. "Journey" implies there is no wrong answer and no starting point that is too late or too embarrassing. Whether they have been active their whole life or have never stepped foot in a gym, this question meets them where they are.

It is broad enough to reveal what matters. When people answer an open question like this, they instinctively lead with what is most important to them. If they start with a health issue, that is what is really driving them. If they start with past gym experience, that context is critical for identifying their Bucket. If they start with a goal, you now have something specific to build toward.

It does not feel like an interview. Compare it to "What are your fitness goals?" — which sounds like a form field — or "Have you been to a gym before?" — which is a yes/no that goes nowhere. "Where are you on your fitness journey?" opens a conversation, not an interrogation.

What to Listen For
As they answer, you are listening on two levels simultaneously. Surface level: the facts (gym history, current activity, stated goals). Deeper level: the emotion (frustration, hope, embarrassment, excitement). The emotion is always more valuable than the fact.
02 The Power of "What Else?" — Digging Past the Surface Going Deeper ▼

Almost every guest gives you a surface answer first. It is not because they are hiding something — it is because that is what comes to mind immediately, and they do not yet know if they can trust you with more. "I just want to get back in shape." "I'm trying to lose a few pounds." These answers are real, but they are not the whole story.

Two words unlock everything underneath: What else?

Surface → "I just want to lose a few pounds and get back into shape."
Layer 2 → "My doctor told me I need to get my blood pressure under control."
X-Factor → "Honestly, I want to be around for my kids. My dad passed from a heart attack at 58 and I'm 46."

That third layer — the one that only came out because you asked "What else?" twice — is the X-Factor. That is the real reason they are here. And the tour you build around that answer is completely different from the tour you would have given based on "I want to lose a few pounds."

The Standard
Ask "What else?" at least twice in every pre-tour. Never stop at the first answer. The surface answer earns a generic tour. The third answer earns a sale.
How NOT to Ask
Do not rush "What else?" like a checklist item. Pause after their answer. Let the silence breathe for a second. Then ask naturally, with genuine curiosity. If it feels like an interrogation they will shut down. If it feels like genuine interest they will open up.
03 The X-Factor Question — Finding the Emotional Driver The Core Skill ▼

The X-Factor is the emotional reason behind the stated goal. It is the why behind the what. And it is the most powerful piece of information you will gather in the entire sales process — because it is the thing that will ultimately move someone from hesitation to commitment.

People do not spend money on gym memberships. They spend money on the person they will become when they use the gym. The X-Factor is that person — and your job is to help them see it clearly.

The X-Factor Question
"If you were to actually achieve these goals — like really accomplish them — what would change in your world? Would you carry yourself differently? Be able to do something you can't do right now? Feel different about yourself?"

This question does several things at once:

It visualizes success. The moment they answer this question, they are mentally living in the outcome. That visualization creates a powerful emotional pull toward action.

It makes the stakes real. Losing 20 pounds is abstract. Being able to keep up with your kids at the park without getting winded is real. The X-Factor converts abstract goals into vivid, personal stakes.

It creates your most powerful closing tool. When objections arise later — price, timing, partners — you come back to this answer. You remind them of what they said they wanted. The X-Factor is the bridge between hesitation and commitment.

The Goal "I want to lose 20 lbs." What they say
The Motivation "My wedding is in 6 months." Why they came in
The X-Factor "I want to feel confident walking down the aisle and in every photo I'll see forever." What closes the sale
Important — Timing
On the T.I., you gather goals but do not push hard for the X-Factor. In the pre-tour, with trust building in person, the X-Factor question lands. Read the room — if rapport is strong, ask it. If they are still guarded, gather more layers through "What else?" first and let it emerge naturally.
04 Open vs. Closed Questions — The Conversation Multiplier Questioning Technique ▼

The type of question you ask determines whether you get a conversation or a dead end. Open-ended questions require thought and produce information. Closed questions produce yes or no — and then you have to ask another question just to get back to zero.

In the pre-tour, open questions are the tool. Closed questions are the trap. Here is how they compare:

✓ Open-Ended
"What have your past gym experiences been like?"
Invites a story. You learn what worked, what did not, what they liked, what they feared. Goldmine.
✗ Closed
"Have you been to a gym before?"
"Yes." Now you have to ask another question. You got one bit of information and no context.
✓ Open-Ended
"What would make this time different from past attempts?"
Forces them to articulate what has held them back — and what they need now to succeed. You just got your entire pitch handed to you.
✗ Closed
"Are you looking to lose weight?"
"Yeah." You learned nothing new. They already told you they want to get in shape. You wasted a question.
✓ Open-Ended
"Tell me more about what you're hoping your day-to-day looks like once you're in a routine."
You get their vision. Their lifestyle. Their schedule preferences. You now know which membership tier fits their life.
✗ Closed
"Do you want to do morning or evening workouts?"
Fine as a clarifying question at the end — but too early here, it cuts off a conversation that would have given you far more useful information.
The Formula
Start with "Tell me...", "What...", "How...", "Walk me through..." — these are your open-ended anchors. Any question that could be answered with one word is a closed question. Rephrase it.
05 Keep Good Notes — The Visible Act of Listening Active Listening ▼

Writing things down during the pre-tour is not just a memory aid. It is one of the most powerful trust-building signals you can send to a guest. When someone sees you writing down what they just said, they receive a clear message: what I am saying matters to this person.

Most people go through life feeling only partially heard. The consultant who actually writes things down — who pauses, nods, and puts pen to paper — stands out immediately as someone who is genuinely engaged.

What to Write Down
Their stated goals. Their X-Factor (word for word if possible — you will use their exact language later). Key past experiences. Any health concerns or limitations. How many days per week they want to train. Specific amenities they react to. Their emotional language.

Here is where the notes pay off: during the tour, when you say "You mentioned earlier that you'd love to finally get to a point where you feel confident going to the beach — let me show you what our members use for that exact goal" — that level of personalization does not happen by accident. It happens because you wrote it down seven minutes ago.

The Callback Technique
Throughout the tour and into the close, refer back to your notes using their exact words. Not a paraphrase — their actual words. "You said you wanted to feel like yourself again." That callback hits differently than a generic benefit statement. It proves you were listening. And people close with consultants who listen.
Do Not Over-Note
You are not a court reporter. You are not looking down at a notepad the entire time. Note key phrases and emotional moments, then look back up and re-engage. Eye contact is still the primary connection tool — notes support it, they do not replace it.
06 Establish Fitness Credibility — The "Did You Know?" Tool Building Trust ▼

You are not just a membership consultant. You are a fitness professional and a guide. One of the fastest ways to reinforce that positioning — without being pushy or showing off — is to drop a relevant, well-timed fitness fact that connects directly to what the guest just shared.

Used sparingly and appropriately, this technique does three things: it shows expertise, it adds value to the conversation, and it shifts the dynamic from sales interaction to trusted advisor conversation.

If They Mention Stress
"Did you know that just 30 minutes of moderate exercise three times a week reduces stress hormones significantly? It works like a natural reset for your nervous system."
If They Mention Sleep Issues
"Did you know regular exercise is one of the most effective non-pharmaceutical interventions for improving sleep quality? Most people notice a difference within two weeks."
If They Mention Weight Loss
"Did you know that increasing your muscle mass actually raises your resting metabolism? You burn more calories at rest the more muscle you carry — even while sleeping."
If They Mention Joint Pain
"Did you know strength training is actually one of the best treatments for chronic joint pain? Strengthening the muscles around a joint reduces the load on the joint itself significantly."
If They Mention Energy
"Did you know regular exercise increases mitochondrial density in your cells? Your body actually produces energy more efficiently the more consistently you train."
If They Mention Getting Older
"Did you know that after 30, we naturally lose muscle mass every decade — but resistance training can almost completely reverse that at any age?"
Two Rules
1. Only use a "Did you know?" when it directly connects to something they just said — never random. 2. Never give specific medical advice or prescribe a treatment for their condition. Keep it general, factual, and empowering.
07 Ask About Past Fitness History — Learn Before You Lead Context ▼

Past gym experiences contain some of the most valuable intelligence you will gather in the entire pre-tour. Someone's history tells you what worked, what failed, what they liked, what scared them away — and critically, what needs to be different this time for them to actually stick with it.

Past History Questions
"Have you been a member somewhere before? What was that experience like?" — "What did you enjoy about it?" — "What made you leave?" — "Is there anything from that experience that felt like a barrier — something you'd want to avoid this time?"

What you are listening for in their answers:

The quitter pattern. Did they leave because of price? Inconvenience? Lack of accountability? Feeling intimidated? Each of these is an objection that is about to resurface — and knowing it in advance means you can address it proactively during the tour rather than reactively during the close.

What lit them up. If they loved group classes at their last gym, you know exactly where to spend extra time on the tour. If they loved a particular piece of equipment, show them yours and upgrade their expectation.

Health considerations. If they mention injuries, surgeries, or health conditions, this is critical information both for safety and for tailoring your program recommendation. Always acknowledge it: "I really appreciate you sharing that — we'll make sure to keep that in mind as we look at the club."

The Flip Technique
Whatever caused them to leave their last gym is your opportunity. "You mentioned the old gym felt really crowded and impersonal — let me show you what we've done here to make it feel completely different." Turn their past pain point into your present differentiator.
08 The Complete Discovery — A Full Pre-Tour in Flow Full Execution ▼

Every skill in this module works together as one fluid conversation. Here is a complete pre-tour discovery executed from start to finish — showing how the questions layer, how "What else?" peels back the surface, and how the X-Factor lands when the timing is right.

Complete Pre-Tour Discovery — Full Dialogue
Consultant"So tell me — where are you on your fitness journey right now?"
Guest"Honestly, I've been pretty off track. I used to work out a lot but I've had a crazy year and I kind of let it go."
Consultant"I hear that — life has a way of getting in the way. What made this the moment you decided to come back?" [Open follow-up — not "what are your goals?"]
Guest"I just feel sluggish. I want to have more energy. And I gained some weight I'm not happy about."
Consultant"Got it — energy and getting back to feeling like yourself." [Writes it down. Reflects their language back.] "What else is on your mind about it?"
Guest"I mean, my doctor brought up my cholesterol at my last appointment. That was kind of a wake-up call."
Consultant"I really appreciate you sharing that. Did you know that consistent aerobic exercise is one of the most effective ways to improve cholesterol levels — sometimes more effective than medication for people who are just starting to see those numbers shift?" [Did You Know — directly relevant.] "Has anything like that come up before, or is this the first time you've really felt that urgency?"
Guest"First time. I kind of ignored it for a while but I've got kids and I can't keep doing that."
Consultant"That makes complete sense." [Pause. Genuine.] "Can I ask — if you actually got this handled, got the routine back, the energy back, the health numbers where they need to be — what would change for you? Like really change, day to day?" [The X-Factor question — timed perfectly.]
Guest"Honestly? I'd just feel like a better dad. I want to be present. I get winded playing with my kids and that's embarrassing."
Consultant"That's a powerful reason." [Writes it down. Word for word.] "Let's make sure this tour is built around exactly that."

Notice what just happened: in under 5 minutes, through natural conversation, this consultant learned the guest's current state, his health trigger, his emotional driver, and his X-Factor. Every stop on the tour now has a purpose. The close has a foundation. And the guest feels genuinely understood — before they have seen a single piece of equipment.

Common Mistakes
What Kills the Discovery

6 Discovery Mistakes
& How to Fix Them

✗ The Mistake
Accepting the first answer and moving on. "I want to lose weight — great, let me show you our cardio area." You just built a tour on a surface answer.
✓ The Fix
Ask "What else?" at least twice. The first answer is the opener. The third answer is the X-Factor. Never stop digging at layer one.
✗ The Mistake
Formulating your response while they are still talking. You stop listening the moment you start thinking about what to say next. The guest feels it immediately.
✓ The Fix
Listen to understand, not to respond. Take a breath after they finish. Then ask your next question. The pause communicates that you actually heard them.
✗ The Mistake
Asking closed questions: "Are you looking to lose weight? Have you been to a gym before? Do you want morning or evening?" Three questions, three one-word answers, zero information.
✓ The Fix
Lead every question with: Tell me... / What... / How... / Walk me through... These force a real answer. One open question beats five closed ones every time.
✗ The Mistake
Pushing the X-Factor question too early — before trust is established. Guest shuts down. "Why is this person asking me about my feelings?"
✓ The Fix
Layer first, then ask. Two rounds of "What else?" plus a "Did you know?" builds enough trust and warmth that the X-Factor question lands as natural curiosity — not an interrogation.
✗ The Mistake
Not writing anything down. You forget key details, give a generic tour, and miss callbacks during the close. The guest feels like one of many — not someone you paid attention to.
✓ The Fix
Write down their exact words. Especially their X-Factor. When you use their language back to them during the close, the effect is powerful. It only happens if you wrote it down.
✗ The Mistake
Talking too much. Filling silence with your own enthusiasm about the club. You just had a 70/30 conversation where you did 70% of the talking. You learned nothing useful.
✓ The Fix
Get comfortable with silence. After asking a question, wait. Resist the urge to fill the gap. The guest will fill it — and what they say in that pause is usually exactly what you needed to hear.
Role-Play Scenarios
Practice

4 Scenarios to
Train On

Practice these until the questions feel completely natural — not scripted. The goal is to be genuinely curious, not performing curiosity. There is a difference and guests can tell.

Scenario 01 — The One-Word Answerer Guarded Guest
Every question you ask gets a short, closed answer. "Where are you on your fitness journey?" → "Just trying to get back in shape." "What else?" → "I don't know, just want to be healthier." They are not hostile — just guarded and not naturally verbose.
Your objective: Do not give up and move to the tour after two flat answers. Change your approach — use more conversational openers: "Tell me more about what 'getting back in shape' looks like for you day to day." Or share a relatable observation: "A lot of people come in after a period of life getting hectic — what's been going on for you?" Make it feel like a conversation between two people, not an intake form.
The one-word answerer is not withholding — they are waiting to see if you are actually interested or just going through the motions. The moment they feel genuine curiosity from you, they open up. Your job is to earn that moment through patience and authentic follow-through.
Scenario 02 — The Oversharer High-Information Guest
This guest answers every question with three minutes of detail. By the time they finish describing their fitness history, you have been at the desk for 15 minutes and have not started the tour yet. They are not being difficult — they are just naturally verbose and highly engaged.
Your objective: Let them talk — but guide the conversation toward what you need. Use active listening signals ("That's really helpful to know") to affirm them, then redirect: "I want to make sure we cover everything — let me ask you one more thing and then I want to show you something I think is going to be really relevant to what you just shared." You are not cutting them off. You are steering with purpose.
The oversharer gives you massive amounts of raw material. Your job is to identify the most emotionally charged thing they said and build your tour around that. Everything else is context. Pick the X-Factor out of the flood and make it the centerpiece.
Scenario 03 — The Health-Driven Guest Medical Motivation
The guest mentions a doctor's recommendation, a recent diagnosis, or a health scare as their primary motivator. They are serious and slightly nervous. This is not a casual lifestyle choice for them — this feels urgent and personal.
Your objective: Acknowledge the gravity of what they shared before moving anywhere else. "I really appreciate you sharing that with me — that takes trust and I want to make sure we take that seriously." Then use a targeted "Did You Know?" that speaks directly to their condition. Build a tour that emphasizes safety, professional guidance (Thrive), and community support. This guest needs to feel safe and capable, not pushed or overwhelmed.
Never give medical advice. Never minimize their health concern. Never say "don't worry about it." Acknowledge, validate, then guide. The health-driven guest often becomes your most loyal, long-term member when they feel genuinely supported from the very first conversation.
Scenario 04 — The "Just Looking" Guest Low Commitment Energy
At the desk, the guest is casual and low-energy: "I'm just checking things out, not in a huge rush to join or anything." They are telling you not to pressure them. They may genuinely be in early research mode, or they may be defensive because they expect a hard sell.
Your objective: Honor their stated pace — then use the discovery process to find out what is actually underneath it. "Totally, no pressure at all — I'm just curious, what made you want to come check this one out specifically?" There is always a reason. Nobody drives to a gym and tours it for no reason. The discovery uncovers that reason, and once you have it, the tour does the work.
The "just looking" posture almost always softens the moment someone feels genuinely heard rather than sold to. Your entire approach in the pre-tour — genuine curiosity, open questions, writing things down — is the antidote to their defensiveness. Do not react to the surface. Work underneath it.
