The Objective

Five Yes Answers
Before the Price.

The price card is not where resistance starts — it is where it surfaces. HACCL's job is to address every non-price objection before pricing is presented, so that when cost comes up, it is the only thing left to navigate. Five questions. Five confirmations. One clear path to the close.

The Core Purpose
HACCL builds psychological momentum. Every "yes" a guest gives makes the next "yes" easier — and the final commitment feel like a natural continuation rather than a leap. By the time you ask "Is there anything other than cost keeping you from joining today?" the answer is almost always no.

01 Why HACCL Exists — The Pre-Close Logic Foundation ▼

Most consultants present the price card and then react to objections. HACCL flips that sequence. It proactively surfaces and resolves every non-price objection before pricing is ever discussed — so that when you sit down with the price sheet, cost is genuinely the only variable left.

Think about the objections that typically follow a price presentation: "The hours don't really work for my schedule." "I'm not sure you have everything I need." "It's a little far from my office." "I'm not sure I'm ready to commit." These are not price objections — they are fit objections. And they should be resolved before the price card comes out, not after.

The Pre-Close Logic
If a guest says "yes" to hours, amenities, convenience, commitment, and liking the club — and then says "I need to think about it" after the price — you know with certainty that cost is the real issue. That clarity makes your objection handling infinitely more focused and effective.

HACCL also does something equally important: it builds a chain of agreement. Each "yes" answer creates a small psychological commitment. People are internally wired toward consistency — once someone has agreed to five things, saying no to a sixth feels inconsistent with everything they just said. The chain of yes answers makes the final commitment the path of least resistance.

When HACCL Happens
Immediately after the tour and the B+F recap — before the price card is touched. This is a non-negotiable sequence. Tour → B+F review → HACCL → Price. Every step in order, every time.
02 The Transition Into HACCL — Setting the Stage The Setup ▼

As you walk back to the desk from the tour, the transition into HACCL should feel seamless — not like a shift into a new phase. The guest should experience it as a natural continuation of the conversation, not a sudden pivot toward closing.

The HACCL Setup Script
"Before we look at memberships, I want to make sure we've covered everything that matters for you. There are five things that make for a perfect member-club pairing — let me just run through them quickly."

This framing is deliberate and important. "Five things that make for a perfect pairing" positions HACCL as a service to the guest — a final quality check to make sure City Fitness is genuinely the right fit — rather than as a closing technique. The guest agrees to the process before it begins, and the questions that follow feel natural rather than scripted.

Tone and Pace
HACCL should be conversational and warm — not rapid-fire. You are confirming things you discussed during the tour, not running through a checklist. Slow down slightly. Make eye contact. Let each answer breathe before moving to the next question. Rushing communicates that you are executing a script. Calm confidence communicates that you genuinely care about getting this right.
Do Not Announce It
Never say "I'm going to do HACCL now" or "let me ask you these five questions." The technique is invisible to the guest. It is simply a thoughtful, natural conversation about whether City Fitness checks all the boxes — which it will, because you spent the last 45 minutes making sure it does.
03 H — Hours: Does the Schedule Work? Letter H ▼

Hours is the first confirmation because it is the most practical. If the club's operating hours do not work with the guest's schedule, nothing else matters. You almost certainly mentioned hours somewhere during the tour — HACCL is where you confirm it landed.

H
Hours
"We talked about hours earlier — do our hours work for your schedule?"
Confirms schedule compatibility before price is discussed. If hours are genuinely an issue, address it now — not after they have said no to the price card.
If they hesitate: "You mentioned you typically work out in the evening — we're open until 11 PM every night. That covers you, right?"
Personalize the Question
Reference what they told you in discovery. If they mentioned early morning workouts: "You said you're a 6 AM person — we're open from 5 AM weekdays. That works for you?" A personalized hours question lands as confirmation; a generic one feels like a form.
04 A, C, C — Amenities, Convenience, Commitment Letters A, C, C ▼

The middle three letters of HACCL address three distinct potential barriers: does the club have what they need, is the location practical, and are they ready to commit. Each question is a targeted objection prevention — and each should be grounded in what they told you on the tour.

A
Amenities
"Based on everything you saw today — does it look like we have everything you need to accomplish your goals?"
This is a direct confirmation that City Fitness covers their fitness needs. After a full tour and B+F review, this should be an easy yes — but it is still worth asking explicitly. A hesitation here tells you something important before the price card appears.
Be specific: "You mentioned wanting Revive access and group classes — you saw both today. Does it look like we've got you covered?" Specific = more powerful than generic.
C
Convenience
"Is our location convenient for you? Whether it's from home, work, or your regular routine?"
Location friction is a silent membership killer. People join gyms they cannot get to consistently — and they quit just as quietly. Addressing convenience now identifies and resolves this before it becomes a reason they walk away or cancel later.
Multi-club angle: "And remember — with Performance+ you have all 8 locations. So if you work in Center City on Tuesdays, there's a club right there too." Location objections disappear with multi-club access framing.
C
Commitment
"You mentioned coming [X] days a week. Are you ready to commit to that?"
This question reinforces their own stated intention and creates an internal commitment before price is discussed. When they say "yes" here, they are not just answering your question — they are making a promise to themselves. That promise carries weight in the close.
Use their number: If they said 4 days a week in discovery, say "4 days a week — are you ready to commit to that?" Reflecting their specific number back makes the commitment feel real, not hypothetical.
The Key to A, C, C
Every question should reference something they specifically told you — not generic versions of the question. "Does it look like we have everything you need?" is okay. "You mentioned wanting a pool and Revive — did you see both of those today?" is powerful. Specificity is the difference.
05 L — Like: The Emotional Confirmation Letter L ▼

The final letter — Like — is the most emotionally direct question in the sequence. After five minutes of confirming practical logistics, this question cuts straight to the gut: do you actually like this place?

L
Like
"Most importantly — do you like the club?"
This question matters for two reasons. First, it gives the guest explicit permission to express their emotional response — which is often the real driver behind the decision. Second, a "yes" here is the most powerful agreement in the sequence. Liking a place is personal, visceral, and hard to argue away from later.
What a strong "yes" sounds like: "Yeah, I really do. It feels different in here." That answer is worth more than any brochure. Acknowledge it: "I'm glad. That feeling is real — it's what our members tell us too."
Why "Like" Comes Last
The emotional question lands harder after five practical confirmations. By the time you ask "do you like it?" the guest has already said yes to hours, amenities, convenience, and commitment. The emotional confirmation is the capstone — and it creates the strongest possible foundation for the final HACCL question that follows.
06 The Final Question — Isolating Cost The Bridge ▼

After five yes answers, you ask one more question. This is the most important question in the entire HACCL sequence — and possibly in the entire sales process. It is not a closing question. It is a diagnostic one.

The Final HACCL Question
"Is there anything other than cost keeping you from becoming a member today?"

This question does something no other question in the process does: it separates price objections from fit objections. If the answer is no — there is nothing else — then you know with absolute certainty that cost is the only barrier. You flip the price card and handle cost directly.

If the answer is yes — there is something else — you have just discovered a hidden objection that would have ambushed you after the price presentation. Now you can address it before price enters the conversation, which is far less complicated than handling two objections simultaneously.

✓"Do our hours work for you?"Yes
✓"Do we have everything you need to accomplish your goals?"Yes
✓"Is our location convenient for your routine?"Yes
✓"Are you ready to commit to [X] days a week?"Yes
✓"Do you like the club?"Yes
→"Is there anything other than cost keeping you from becoming a member today?"No
What "No" Means
When they say no — there is nothing else — you have created a clean, isolated price conversation. Everything else is resolved. The only question now is whether the value justifies the cost. And you have already built that case with B+F Logic. Now you flip the price card.
What If They Say "Yes, There Is Something Else"?
That is valuable intelligence. Ask: "What is it?" Address it directly and fully before moving to price. Common hidden objections: partner needs to see it, not sure about commitment length, concerns about a health issue. Handle each with LIGO principles. Then re-ask the final question before presenting price.

Common Mistakes
What Breaks the Bridge

6 HACCL Mistakes
& How to Fix Them

✗ The Mistake
Skipping HACCL entirely and going straight from the tour to the price card. You hit a price objection that is actually a convenience or schedule issue — and you have no foundation to work from.
✓ The Fix
Tour → B+F → HACCL → Price. Every time. The sequence is non-negotiable. HACCL takes under 3 minutes and prevents objections that would take 10 minutes to handle reactively.
✗ The Mistake
Asking generic HACCL questions with no personalization: "Do we have what you need?" instead of "You said you wanted Revive and the pool — did you see both today?"
✓ The Fix
Every question references something they specifically told you. Generic HACCL feels like a checklist. Personalized HACCL feels like genuine confirmation. The difference is your notes from discovery.
✗ The Mistake
Rushing through all five questions like a rapid-fire checklist. The guest feels like they are being processed, not consulted.
✓ The Fix
Conversational pace. Let each answer breathe. Acknowledge a strong yes with a brief response before moving on. The warmth between questions is what makes it feel like a dialogue, not a script.
✗ The Mistake
Skipping the final question — "Is there anything other than cost?" — and going straight to price after the five letters. You leave hidden objections buried under the price conversation.
✓ The Fix
Always ask the final question. It is the most diagnostically powerful question in the process. A "yes, there is something else" before the price card is a gift — not a setback.
✗ The Mistake
When they reveal a hidden objection to the final question, panicking and going to price anyway. Now you are managing two problems at once.
✓ The Fix
Stop. Address the hidden objection fully using LIGO. Resolve it completely, then re-ask the final question. Only when the answer is "no, nothing else" do you proceed to price.
✗ The Mistake
Treating the commitment question as hypothetical: "Do you think you'd come 4 times a week?" Hedged language invites hedged answers.
✓ The Fix
"You said 4 days a week — are you ready to commit to that?" Direct language creates a real commitment. Use their number. Ask if they are ready. The word "commit" matters — use it.
Role-Play Scenarios
Practice

4 HACCL Scenarios
to Train On

Scenario 01 — Clean Run, All Yes Answers Standard
Guest had a great tour, loved the club, showed strong interest throughout. You expect five clean yes answers and a smooth bridge to the price card.
Your objective: Execute the full HACCL sequence with warmth and confidence. Personalize every question using their discovery answers. Do not rush — even with a highly engaged guest, let each yes land before moving on. Practice the transition from the final question directly into the price presentation without any awkward pause or bridging language. "Perfect. Then let's look at memberships." Clean. Confident. Done.
The clean run is where you build the muscle memory. Practice it until the transition from HACCL to price card feels completely natural. The goal is that the guest experiences the whole sequence as one continuous, comfortable conversation — not a sales process with identifiable phases.
Scenario 02 — Hesitation on Commitment Objection Surfacing
Everything is going smoothly until the commitment question: "You mentioned 3–4 days a week — are you ready to commit to that?" The guest pauses: "I want to, but I've said that before and it hasn't worked out."
Your objective: Do not skip past this hesitation. This is a Start & Stopper speaking honestly about their pattern — and it is important. Acknowledge it genuinely: "I really appreciate you being honest about that. Let me ask you — what made the difference the other times you've tried? What would make this time different?" Use their answer to reinforce how City Fitness specifically addresses that gap (community, accountability, Thrive coaching). Re-ask commitment after the conversation, then continue HACCL.
A hesitation on commitment during HACCL is one of the most valuable moments in the entire interaction. The guest is telling you exactly what they need to hear. Your job is to listen, address it with specifics, and help them see that the barriers that stopped them before do not exist here in the same way.
Scenario 03 — Hidden Objection Surfaces Final Question Response
Five yes answers — clean. You ask the final question: "Is there anything other than cost keeping you from becoming a member today?" They pause and say: "I'd really like my wife to see it before I commit to anything."
Your objective: Do not go to the price card. This is a real objection that needs handling before pricing. Apply LIGO: Listen fully. Identify (it's about inclusion, not permission). Give permission ("I completely understand — major decisions are better made together"). Offer alternative (the partner LIGO script — "If your wife were here today and heard everything you just told me you loved about this place... what do you think she'd say?"). Work through it fully. Re-ask the final question only when genuinely resolved.
This is exactly why the final HACCL question exists. Without it, this objection arrives at the worst possible moment — after the price card is on the table. Discovered before pricing, it is a solvable conversation. Discovered after, it is a much harder close. HACCL earns its keep in moments exactly like this one.
Scenario 04 — Convenience Hesitation Location Objection
During the convenience question, the guest says: "It's a little farther than I was hoping. I was really looking for something walkable from my apartment."
Your objective: Address this before it becomes a reason to leave. First, confirm how far they actually are — often guests overestimate distance. Second, apply the multi-club argument: "With Performance+, you have access to all 8 City Fitness locations — are any of those closer to your apartment?" If yes, pivot the conversation to that location as their home club. If no location is closer, reframe: "For a lot of members, the commute becomes part of the routine — 10 minutes each way is 20 minutes less mindless scrolling." Resolve it, confirm convenience, and continue.
Location objections surface during HACCL or after pricing — and they are almost always solvable. Multi-club access is your strongest tool. Most guests are unaware of all 8 locations or where they are. A map pulled up on your phone showing the closest location to their home or office has closed this objection more times than any verbal argument.
