The Framework

Three Buckets.
One Question.

Everything starts with: "Where are you on your fitness journey?" The answer tells you exactly which bucket they are in — and which bucket determines how you speak, what you show, and what you emphasize for the rest of the interaction.

Bucket 01
Avid Exerciser
Regular exercise history. Self-motivated. Knows the language. Looking to upgrade, break plateaus, or access better equipment and programming.
Bucket 02
Start & Stopper
Has tried before. Knows what a gym is. Struggles with consistency and accountability. Needs a different reason to believe this time is different.
Bucket 03
Never Fitness
Little to no gym experience. Intimidated. Needs safety, simplicity, and a welcoming environment before anything else.
The Point of the Buckets
The Buckets Theory exists to kill the museum tour — the generic, feature-by-feature walkthrough that ignores who the person actually is. A tour built for an Avid Exerciser delivered to a Never Fitness guest will overwhelm and lose them. A Never Fitness tour delivered to an Avid Exerciser will bore and insult them. The bucket determines everything.

01 How to Identify the Bucket — Within 5 Minutes Identification ▼

You do not need a form or a checklist to identify the bucket. You need to listen carefully to how someone answers "Where are you on your fitness journey?" and ask one or two follow-up questions. The language they use, the confidence they carry, and what they lead with will tell you everything within the first 3–5 minutes.

Avid Exerciser
"I've been lifting for years but I've hit a plateau on my squat..."
Uses fitness terminology. Talks about specifics. Self-aware about their training.
Start & Stopper
"I keep starting and then life gets in the way. I've had memberships before..."
Acknowledges past attempts. Slightly apologetic. Looking for something different.
Never Fitness
"I've never really done this before. I don't really know where to start..."
Uncertain language. Admits inexperience. Looking for reassurance first, information second.
The 5-Minute Standard
You should know the bucket within 5 minutes of sitting down. If you are still unsure after 5 minutes, you are either not asking the right follow-up questions or not listening to the signals they are sending. Speed here matters — the sooner you know, the sooner the tour becomes personal.
The Bucket Is a Guide, Not a Box
The buckets are a framework for calibration, not a label. Some people are between buckets. Some will surprise you. Stay flexible — use the bucket to set your starting point, then adjust as the conversation develops. The goal is a personalized experience, not a perfectly sorted database entry.
02 Bucket 1: The Avid Exerciser — Expert to Expert Bucket 01 ▼

This guest has been in gyms before. They know what a rack is, what a PR means, and how to use most of the equipment without instruction. They are not here to be taught the basics — they are here to be impressed. Treat them as a peer, not a beginner.

Proud of Their History
They will reference past achievements. Acknowledge and respect it. Do not minimize it by moving on too quickly.
Knows the Language
You can use fitness terminology freely. Do not over-explain basic concepts — it signals that you underestimated them.
Has Specific Goals
Not "get fit" — something specific. A race time, a lift number, a physique goal. Focus your tour around exactly that.
Self-Motivated
They do not need convincing to work out. They need convincing that City Fitness is worth switching to. Different sales job entirely.
May Have Hit a Plateau
Stagnation is a common reason avid exercisers switch gyms. New equipment, programming, and Revive recovery are your differentiators here.
Authenticity Matters
If they bring up something you do not know — admit it. "I'm not an expert in that area, but we have coaches who specialize in exactly that." Honesty builds more trust than bluffing.
What to Emphasize on the Tour — Avid Exerciser
Advanced equipment and free weight selection — show them what they cannot get elsewhere
Revive recovery suite — cryo, infrared sauna, red light, Normatec — this is a massive differentiator for serious athletes
Specialized studio classes — metric-based cycling, WE/Fit functional training — programming built for people who take fitness seriously
Thrive assessment — frame it as data and optimization, not beginner guidance
Multi-club access — the convenience of having eight locations is a practical upgrade for someone already committed to regular training
The Avid Exerciser Script Approach
"Based on what you're describing, it sounds like you've already got the discipline locked in — what you need is an environment that matches that level. Let me show you what we've built for exactly that."
03 Bucket 2: The Start & Stopper — Structure and Support Bucket 02 ▼

This is your most common prospect. They have tried before. They know what a gym is. They have had memberships, started routines, and then — life happened. They quit. They know they are capable of being consistent. They just have not been able to maintain it, and they carry a layer of frustration and mild embarrassment about that.

Your entire approach with this person must answer one unspoken question: Why is this time going to be different? Everything you show them and say to them needs to point toward an answer to that question.

Has Tried Before
They know the basics. Do not over-explain equipment. Treat them as someone who just needs the right environment, not the right education.
Struggled With Consistency
The pattern is familiar to them. Acknowledge it without shame. "A lot of our members come in exactly where you are right now."
Needs Accountability
This is the #1 gap for Start & Stoppers. Thrive coaching, studio classes, and workout buddies are your accountability tools. Lead with them.
May Have Health Concerns
Often restarting due to a doctor's visit or a health scare. Take it seriously. Acknowledge it. This is often the X-Factor hiding under the surface.
Needs to Feel Different
They are not just choosing a gym — they are choosing to believe this time is different. Your job is to show them the specific things that make that true.
Deep "What Else?" Required
The surface answer will always be a vague goal. Dig. Ask what has held them back before. The answer to that question tells you exactly what to sell.
What to Emphasize on the Tour — Start & Stopper
Thrive coaching program — professional guidance, accountability, and a plan. This is the structural missing piece for most Start & Stoppers.
Studio classes and community — showing up for a class is harder to skip than a solo workout. Community = accountability.
Introduce them to staff — a face-to-face connection with a Thrive coach or manager makes the club feel like a place where people know them.
The welcoming environment — emphasize that this is a place for real people at every level, not an intimidating performance gym.
Ask what made them stop before — then show them specifically what is different here. Turn their past barrier into your present differentiator.
The Start & Stopper Script Approach
"You mentioned you've tried to get consistent before and life got in the way — that's incredibly common. The difference here is that you won't be doing it alone. Let me show you what we've built specifically for that."
Handling the "I've Tried Before" Moment
Guest"I've had memberships before and honestly I just stopped going. I don't really know what's going to be different this time."
Consultant"I really appreciate you being honest about that — and honestly that's the most common thing I hear. Most people don't stop going because they lost the desire. They stop because they didn't have the right structure around them."
Consultant"What usually triggered it for you — was it schedule, was it feeling like you didn't know what to do when you got here, or something else?"
Guest"Mostly schedule. And I'd get there and kind of wander around not knowing what to do."
Consultant"That's exactly what I want to show you we solve here. Come on back."
04 Bucket 3: Never Fitness — Safety and Vision First Bucket 03 ▼

This person has little to no gym experience. They may have never been in a fitness facility before, or their experience is so minimal that it barely counts. Walking in here took courage — and they may not look like it on the surface, but they are in a vulnerable state.

Your entire approach must lead with safety, simplicity, and possibility. Everything you say should lower the barrier, not raise the bar. This is not the time to showcase your most advanced equipment. This is the time to make someone believe they belong here.

No Assumption of Knowledge
Zero fitness jargon. Do not say "selectorized," "metabolic conditioning," or "periodization." If you need to explain a machine, do it in plain English.
Intimidated by the Environment
A room full of people who know what they are doing can feel overwhelming to someone who does not. Normalize it. Make it feel approachable.
Needs to Dream First
Before features, before pricing — they need to visualize themselves here. Paint the picture of what their life could look like once fitness becomes a habit.
Requires Patient Pacing
They may have many questions. They may process slowly. Never rush them. A rushed Never Fitness guest is a lost sale every time.
Personal Connection Critical
Introduce them to staff by name early. A familiar face makes a gym feel safe. They need to know there are real people here who will help them.
The X-Factor Is Everything
There is always a powerful reason someone with no gym history finally walked in. Find it. It is usually emotional, personal, and significant. Build everything around it.
What to Emphasize on the Tour — Never Fitness
The welcoming atmosphere — the first thing they need to feel is that they will not be judged here. Say it explicitly if you have to.
Thrive consultation — frame it as "we build a plan just for you so you never have to wonder what to do when you get here." This directly addresses their biggest fear.
Simple, accessible areas first — do not open with the advanced barbell area. Start where they can picture themselves.
Introduce staff by name — a real personal connection with a Thrive coach or manager transforms "a gym" into "my gym."
Keep the tour focused — do not overwhelm with every amenity. Hit the highlights that speak to their stated goals and leave the rest for after they join.
The Never Fitness Script Approach
"First thing I want you to know — everybody here started exactly where you are right now. There is no wrong starting point. Our job is just to make sure you never have to figure this out alone."
The Jargon Trap
The fastest way to lose a Never Fitness guest is to make them feel stupid. Using terminology they do not understand without explanation does exactly that. When in doubt, speak to a friend who is curious about fitness for the first time — that is your target voice for this bucket.
05 Language Calibration — Speaking Their Language Communication ▼

The bucket does not just change what you show on the tour. It changes how you speak. The words you choose, the terms you use, the assumptions you make — all of it needs to calibrate to where this person is. Speaking over someone's head loses them. Speaking beneath them insults them. The bucket tells you exactly where to pitch your language.

Same Feature — Three Different Languages
For Avid"Our Revive suite includes Normatec compression, cryo chamber, and infrared — serious athletes are using this daily for recovery optimization between sessions."
For Start/Stop"We have a recovery area that helps your body bounce back faster — so you are not as sore and you actually want to come back the next day. A lot of people find it becomes one of their favorite parts of the membership."
For Never"We have this amazing area where you can recover after a workout — there is heat therapy, cold therapy, and compression. Think of it as a spa that makes your body feel better after you exercise. You would be surprised how much you end up looking forward to it."

The same amenity. The same truth. Three completely different deliveries — each one calibrated to where the person is. None of them are dumbed down or inflated. They are just right-sized for the audience.

The Language Rule
Match their vocabulary, not yours. If they use a term, you can use it. If they do not use a term, explain it before you use it — or skip it entirely and describe the outcome instead. Outcomes are universally understood. Terminology is not.
06 The Museum Tour — What You Are Trying to Avoid Anti-Pattern ▼

The museum tour is what happens when a consultant ignores the bucket entirely and just walks every guest through the same route in the same order with the same scripted lines. It is the default mode for undertrained or disengaged consultants — and it is the single biggest tour-level reason for lost sales.

It looks like this: start at the front desk, walk left past the cardio deck, point out the weight room, mention the studios, gesture at the pool, end at the membership desk. Same for everyone. Feature after feature. No personalization. No connection to what the person actually cares about.

Why the Museum Tour Fails
The guest feels like a number moving through a process. They are not inspired. They are not emotionally connected. And when it comes time to close, there is nothing personal to anchor the conversation to. The bucket-based tour makes every stop feel like it was chosen specifically for them — because it was.

Compare these two closings:

Museum Tour Close vs. Bucket-Based Tour Close
Museum"So you saw everything — we have cardio, weights, classes, and the pool. What do you think?"
Bucket-Based"Based on everything you shared about wanting to finally feel consistent and having someone in your corner — we showed you the Thrive program, you met Coach [Name], and you saw the class schedule that fits your mornings. I think we've got exactly what makes the difference for you. Let's make it official."

One of those closes connects. One does not. The bucket is what made the difference — and it started at the very beginning of the pre-tour, not at the membership desk.

07 Between Buckets — When It Is Not Obvious Edge Cases ▼

Not every guest fits neatly into one bucket. Some are on the border. Some will surprise you mid-tour by revealing more than you expected. Some Start & Stoppers have more fitness knowledge than their hesitancy suggests. Some Avid Exercisers are secretly more fragile about their consistency than they let on. Here is how to handle the edge cases.

When they seem like a Start & Stopper but talk like an Avid Exerciser: Meet them where their confidence is — use appropriate terminology, acknowledge their knowledge — but still build accountability and structure into your pitch. They have the knowledge. What they need is the system.

When they seem like an Avid Exerciser but mention significant inconsistency: Do not assume. Ask: "It sounds like you have a solid foundation — what has gotten in the way of staying consistent?" That answer will recalibrate your approach immediately.

When you genuinely cannot tell: Start closer to the middle — respectful of fitness knowledge, warm about the supportive environment. And keep asking questions. The bucket will reveal itself. It always does.

The Adjustment Rule
You can change buckets mid-tour. If new information comes out that reframes who this person is, adjust. The bucket is a starting point, not a locked-in decision. The consultant who adapts in real time builds more trust than the one who sticks rigidly to their first read.
The Universal Truth Across All Buckets
Regardless of which bucket someone is in — the X-Factor, active listening, genuine curiosity, and a tour built around their specific life are non-negotiable. The bucket changes the delivery. It never changes the standard of care.

What Kills the Personalization

6 Bucket Mistakes
& How to Fix Them

✗ The Mistake
Giving every guest the same tour regardless of who they are. The museum tour. Feature after feature. No personalization. No connection.
✓ The Fix
Identify the bucket within 5 minutes. Every stop on the tour should feel like it was chosen specifically for this person — because it was.
✗ The Mistake
Using fitness jargon with a Never Fitness guest. "Let's check out our selectorized circuit and functional training turf." They have no idea what that means.
✓ The Fix
Describe outcomes in plain language. "This area has machines that help you build strength in different parts of your body — and there are instructional videos right on them so you always know what to do."
✗ The Mistake
Treating an Avid Exerciser like a beginner. Over-explaining basics, showing them introductory equipment. They feel condescended to and you lose credibility immediately.
✓ The Fix
Match their level. Speak peer to peer. Go straight to the advanced equipment, the recovery suite, the programming that matches their goals. Show them the upgrade.
✗ The Mistake
Skipping accountability and structure for a Start & Stopper. Showing them equipment when what they actually need is a reason to believe this time is different.
✓ The Fix
Lead with the system. Thrive coaching. Studio classes. Staff introductions. Community. Show them the accountability architecture that has been missing every time before.
✗ The Mistake
Rushing a Never Fitness guest through the tour. They need time to process, ask questions, and feel safe. Speed communicates pressure — and pressure makes them leave.
✓ The Fix
Slow down. Answer every question thoroughly. For a Never Fitness guest, the moment they feel rushed is the moment they decide to "think about it."
✗ The Mistake
Locking in a bucket read and never adjusting. New information comes out mid-tour. You ignore it because you already decided who this person is.
✓ The Fix
Stay flexible. The bucket is a starting point. If new information changes the picture, adjust immediately. The best consultants recalibrate in real time.
Role-Play Scenarios
Practice

4 Scenarios — One
Per Bucket Plus a Curveball

Practice identifying the bucket from the opening answer and adjusting your entire approach accordingly. Speed and accuracy are both part of the standard.

Scenario 01 — The Competitive Athlete Bucket 01 · Avid Exerciser
"I've been running competitively for three years and I'm getting more into strength training. My last gym just didn't have the equipment I needed for the programming my coach gave me."
Your objective: Immediately match their level. No basics. Go straight to the free weight selection, the strength training area, and Revive recovery — frame all of it around performance enhancement and recovery between training blocks. Ask about their specific programming so you can show them exactly what applies. Introduce Thrive as a performance optimization tool, not a beginner consultation.
The competitive athlete has a high bar for credibility. If you over-explain something they already know, you lose ground instantly. Stay in your lane — speak to what City Fitness offers, and if they go deep on sport-specific programming you are not expert in, say so and connect them to someone who is.
Scenario 02 — The Returning Member Bucket 02 · Start & Stopper
"I was actually a member here about two years ago. I got really consistent for a while and then work got crazy and I just stopped coming. I feel bad about it honestly."
Your objective: Remove the guilt immediately — normalize it, validate it, and move forward. Ask what was working when they were consistent and what specifically triggered the fall-off. Use that answer to show them what is different or what they can put in place now to prevent the same pattern. Accountability structures are your pitch — Thrive, classes, a community that notices when you are gone.
The returning member is actually your warmest Start & Stopper lead. They already know City Fitness. They liked it enough to come back. Your job is simply to give them a better reason to stay this time. Find out what broke down before and sell the solution to exactly that.
Scenario 03 — The First-Timer Bucket 03 · Never Fitness
"My doctor told me I need to start exercising. I've honestly never been in a gym before. I don't really know what I'm supposed to do and I don't want to look stupid in front of everyone."
Your objective: Safety and belonging before everything else. Acknowledge the courage it took to walk in. Normalize their experience — "Most people feel exactly that way the first time." Introduce Thrive as the answer to "I don't know what to do" — frame it as a professional who builds a plan just for them so they never have to guess. Keep the tour focused, avoid overwhelming them with the full facility, and introduce a staff member by name to create a personal anchor point.
This guest is one genuine moment of connection away from being your most loyal long-term member. They came in vulnerable and they need to leave feeling capable and welcome. The close for this person is not price — it is belief. Sell the belief first.
Scenario 04 — The Curveball Between Buckets
"I used to be really into fitness — like seriously into it, competed and everything. But that was ten years ago. I've barely touched a gym since then. I'm not sure where I even am anymore."
Your objective: Do not assume either bucket. This person has the knowledge of an Avid Exerciser and the current state of a Start & Stopper. Ask: "What does getting back into it look like for you — are you thinking about picking up where you left off or starting fresh?" Their answer tells you which direction to lean. Likely a hybrid approach: respect their history and knowledge, but build in accountability and a structured re-entry plan through Thrive.
The ten-years-away former athlete is one of the most interesting prospects you will meet. Their identity is still tied to fitness — they just lost the path back. Your job is to be the bridge. Acknowledge who they were, meet them where they are now, and show them the route forward.
